How ready is your property to let?

Answer eight quick questions and we’ll show you what’s done, what’s missing, and how to sort it.

1. Do you have a Gas Safety record from the last 12 months?

Every gas appliance, fitting and flue must be checked every year by a Gas Safe registered engineer.

2. Has the electrics had an EICR in the last 5 years, with a satisfactory result?

Private landlords in England must have the electrical installation inspected and tested at least every 5 years.

3. Is there a valid EPC rated A to E?

You need an EPC to let a property. It lasts 10 years, and the minimum rating to let is currently E (some exemptions apply).

4. Is there a working smoke alarm on every floor, and a carbon monoxide alarm where needed?

A smoke alarm on each storey with living space, and a CO alarm in any room with a fixed combustion appliance (not gas cookers). Check they work on the first day of the tenancy.

5. Does the property have the licence it needs from the council?

Many London councils require a licence for some or all rented homes (selective, additional or HMO licensing).

6. Will you have an inventory done at check-in?

Not a legal requirement, but it’s your main evidence if there’s a deposit dispute.

7. If you take a deposit, will it be protected in a government scheme within 30 days?

Deposits must be protected in an approved scheme within 30 days, and the tenant given the prescribed information.

8. Will you check every adult tenant’s right to rent before the tenancy starts?

Landlords in England must check that every adult who will live in the property has the right to rent.

What landlords need to know

The essentials, in plain English

General information for private landlords in England, last reviewed October 2026. Rules change — always check the official guidance linked below. This isn’t legal advice.

Gas safety

Every 12 months

Have every gas appliance, fitting and flue checked by a Gas Safe registered engineer. Give your tenant a copy of the record within 28 days of the check (and before a new tenant moves in), and keep records for 2 years.

Book a Gas Safety check →HSE: gas safety for landlords

Electrical safety (EICR)

At least every 5 years

Have the electrical installation inspected and tested by a qualified person. Give your tenant a copy of the report within 28 days, and do any required repairs within 28 days (or sooner if the report says so).

Book an EICR →GOV.UK: electrical safety standards

Energy Performance Certificate (EPC)

Valid for 10 years

You need a valid EPC before you market or let the property, and must give a copy to your tenant. The current minimum rating to let is E, with some exemptions. The government has proposed raising this, so check GOV.UK for the latest.

Book an EPC →GOV.UK: minimum energy efficiency standard

Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms

Check on day one

A smoke alarm on every storey with living space, and a carbon monoxide alarm in any room with a fixed combustion appliance (except gas cookers). Make sure they work at the start of each tenancy and repair them when told they’re faulty.

GOV.UK: smoke and CO alarm guidance

Property licensing

Depends on the council

Many London councils license some or all private rented homes. Letting without a licence you need can mean fines and repaying rent. Check your address, and we can apply for you free of charge (council fee separate).

Inventory and check-in

Every new tenancy

Not legally required, but it records the condition of the property and is your main evidence if there’s a disagreement about the deposit. Your tenant can complete our DIY inventory on their phone for £30 + VAT.

Within 30 days

Protect any deposit in a government-approved scheme within 30 days of receiving it, and give your tenant the prescribed information.

Assess the risk
